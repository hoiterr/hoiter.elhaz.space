The data isn't the question — trust in it is. Finance has no audit trail. Depends on finance access levels, but if they operate within the payout system for invoices, finance only receive a completed invoice with no attached audit trail — no deal reference, no change history, no link back to what was agreed. That's the visibility gap.
// Part 02

Proposal

Fix the data first; automate second. The order matters more than the tooling.

Step 1 — Drop the Slack workflow

Make AEs update HubSpot instead of submitting forms in Slack.

Step 2 — Audit and harden the deal object

Audit the deal creation process, deal stages, account info and tracking within these objects. Train AEs to use HubSpot as the source of truth, and make the most crucial fields mandatory to enter the Billing stage. HubSpot doesn't do Salesforce-style field-by-field validation rules, but it does support stage gating — required properties on a deal stage. So if Currency (or Amount, or Product line) is empty, the AE simply can't move the deal into Billing until it's filled. Because Billing is also the automation trigger, the quality gate and the trigger become the same moment — a deal can't generate an invoice unless it's complete. When the data inside HubSpot is trustworthy, tested, verified and fresh, then we build automations on top of it.

Data good? Good. Now we can roll into direct answers that lay out the general direction.

Direct Answers

Answers to the task questions, given the proposal above.

1
How does HubSpot data feed invoice generation? Let's say we use n8n webhooks, pull deal properties directly, and generate a formatted invoice in Tipalti. So clean data on Currency, Amount, Dates from–to, Product line, and other info we need to configure from the deal page is reflected on the invoice.
2
What triggers the process? A deal object status-change watcher — though the right trigger depends on how Hostinger actually bills partners, which I'd confirm first. If it's one deal per invoice, a deal stage change to Billing is the easiest, cleanest trigger: the AE moves the deal, the automation fires. If partners are billed per period and several deals roll into one payout, a per-deal trigger doesn't fit — you'd get a separate invoice per deal — so instead a scheduled period-end job runs when the billing period closes, scans all billable deals per partner account, and aggregates them into one invoice (see next answer). I leaned on the stage-change model because I don't yet know the billing cadence here — in a previous company it was one deal per account with recurring, future-dated deals, a different model. Which one applies is the key discovery question.
3
Multiple deals on one invoice? Depends whether partners are billed per deal or per period. An invoice has a header (partner, billing period, totals) and line items — one row per charge. Billing tools like Tipalti support multiple line items natively, so a partner with, say, five deals closing in the same period gets one invoice with five line items, each tied to its deal ID — not five separate invoices. The aggregation happens before the invoice is created: the scheduled period-end job (or n8n) groups that partner's billable deals by partner + period, then creates the single Tipalti bill. This cuts invoice volume and payout overhead for finance and the partner alike.
4
Detecting mismatches? If my process is followed, there's no "requested ≠ generated" mismatch — the invoice is built from the same deal data, so there's no separate form to disagree with the CRM. The remaining risk is a different one: garbage in. If an AE enters the wrong number in HubSpot itself, the invoice faithfully reproduces it. To close that gap we'd need to know where the original agreement actually lands — email, a call, a meeting — and whether we can capture or correlate that source against the deal before the invoice generates. Where we can't, PartnerOps owns the edge-case review — talking to AEs, resolving in HubSpot — not finance.
5
Writeback to HubSpot? Best case scenario: the integration we use has automatic deal-stage statuses, or at least some field update on the deal/account object when an invoice has been paid out (e.g. a successful payout timestamp), based on which we can trigger a deal status change to Paid or update other fields. If that's not an option, finance would undergo a simple CRM training on how to update the HubSpot deal status. (We'd create workflows so that when status is updated to Paid, it timestamps when it was paid out, etc.)
6
Would one third-party tool solve it? I guess this depends on how complex the billing is and how much the AEs deviate with custom pricing. The more variability, the less automation we can build — but as per quick market research, it seems Tipalti ↗ could potentially fulfil all needs.

Questions & Recommendations

The gaps I'd close before designing anything — grouped by who I'd ask.

Partnerships
◎How does the day-to-day account-exec activity look? How much of the data entry, data hygiene, and CRM usage do they actually do throughout the day? How can we improve that? Do they feel they're wasting time entering information into HubSpot? Generally, how do they actually operate within the CRM boundaries?
Finance
◎What is the payout structure for partner invoices? How does the payment model look, and how complex is it?
◎When the complete invoice is generated and finance is notified via email, does the team go through any final checks, or does it simply go through a payout process and that's it?
◎Does the team have access to HubSpot? What visibility is currently available, and what are the current pitfalls of billing?
PartnerOps
◎How does the partner ops team operate within these invoice tasks? What parts of this are automated? How much time does partner ops spend per invoice? How many invoices are we billing monthly? What part of the invoice building costs the most time, and why does it cost what it does?
Operations / Admin
◎What is the current deal process? How many stages do we have? How many deals can one partner have at the moment? Do we split deals per specific product? How does deal management work in general? Are there any automations around deal creation, renewals, recurring payments?
◎How much manual CRM entry work can we assign to AEs and BDRs?
◎When submitting invoice requests, does the AE have to re-enter all of the information in the form itself? How does the process actually look from the AE's perspective? Do we HAVE to keep the form?
◎What's the company's "feel" on CRM data — accurate, in the middle, complete BS — and why?
◎Change management within the organization: the new process of dropping Slack — big hurdle or easy cruise? Any history-proven ways to solve this and move as quickly as possible?
◎When buying new tools, is there a security compliance and audit requirement? What's the new-tool acquisition process?
